Goal #1 · Identify the language, then translate

We can partly sound out Linear A.
We still can't understand it.

Linear A's successor script, Linear B, was deciphered in 1952 — and because many Linear A signs share shapes with Linear B signs of known sound, we can often plausibly "sound out" Linear A sign-sequences. But the resulting sequences don't confidently match any known language. Our first goal is to test that gap rigorously. Our second is to document every step here. Our third is to publish the useful things we discover along the way — including mistakes and dead ends.

We can partly sound it out. We still can't understand it.

A different starting line than our sibling projects.

Unlike Voynich or rongorongo, the open question here is not "what kind of system is this" — Linear A is accepted as a syllabary closely related to the deciphered Linear B script. Many of its signs already have phonetic values borrowed from Linear B. The central open question is what language those sounded-out sequences actually encode — and whether the borrowed sign values themselves are even reliable for signs Linear B never used. See the sidequest queue for the full plan.
